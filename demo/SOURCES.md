San Francisco and Southgate were confirmed anonymously accessible (no token, no cookies) via `curl -s --compressed <SceneServer>/layers/0?f=json`, returning HTTP 200 with no `error`/"Token Required" body, and both were checked for the `uvRegion` vertex attribute in `store.defaultGeometrySchema.vertexAttributes` (as `region`; present on both, `UInt16` x4) plus `textureSetDefinitions[].atlas === true`. Southgate's `spatialReference.wkid` is `102100`, so it doubles as the Web Mercator projected-layer example; San Francisco 3D Buildings is `wkid 4326` and stands alone as the atlas-UV example. Both are `layerType` `3DObject`.

Scene_Layers is Esri's own sample-content account, and Wellington City Council is a New Zealand municipal open-data publisher with an explicit CC BY 4.0 licence.

The tiling-UV layer was found and measured with `node scripts/find-tiling-uv-layers.mjs <SceneServer>`: it reads node page 0 (plus the page holding the largest leaf's parent), fetches three uncompressed geometry buffers and parses uv0 and uvRegion. On Boston_DotAve_CE_Example_WSL1 62.6% of 204,030 sampled vertices lie in a real atlas sub-rectangle (e.g. 0.039 x 0.312 of the atlas) with uv0 outside [0,1] (u up to 83.5, v down to -44.1), so stock CesiumJS wraps them into neighbouring atlas slots. One of the three sampled nodes was the same node counted twice; with it counted once the share is about 57%, which does not change the conclusion. San Francisco and Southgate measured 0% on the same check, which is why they render identically with and without the fix. The layer returned HTTP 200 anonymously (no token, no cookies); layerType `3DObject`, I3S 1.8, every `textureSetDefinitions[].atlas` is `true`, and `uvRegion` is present in `geometryDefinitions[].geometryBuffers[0]`. Its owner belongs to the Esri R&D Center Zurich organisation (CityEngine's developers).
